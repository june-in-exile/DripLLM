# DripLLM

Time-metered LLM inference streaming. The buyer gets inference only while it keeps paying; stop paying and the seller cuts the line. This document defines vocabulary only — it does not record implementation decisions.

## Money

Four different numbers in this project tend to get loosely called "balance". They move at completely different moments, and mixing them up leads straight to misunderstandings or lost funds.

**Wallet balance**:
The USDC the buyer's address holds on-chain. It changes only at two moments — depositing and refunding — and does not move at all during a stream.
_Avoid_: balance, balance (on its own)

**Deposit**:
USDC the buyer has pre-loaded into the payment channel contract and not yet spent. It decreases tick by tick during a stream; it is the number the user is actually burning.
_Avoid_: balance, channel balance, credit, top-up

**Consumed**:
The amount the seller has already served and is owed in total. It only ever goes up; an on-chain claim does not reset it to zero.
_Avoid_: charged, paid

**Authorized cap**:
The cumulative cap the buyer signed on its latest voucher, i.e. the buyer's current exposure. It authorizes the seller to collect _at most_ this amount; it does not represent how much service was actually delivered.
_Avoid_: quota, limit, allowance

## Billing

**Tick**:
The smallest billing unit: one slice of streaming time the buyer buys with a single payment.
_Avoid_: cycle, period, slot

**Voucher**:
An off-chain credential the buyer signs once per tick. It records a cumulative cap, not the per-tick amount. It never goes on-chain and costs no gas.
_Avoid_: receipt, IOU, credential

**Exposure window**:
The time between the buyer stopping payment and the seller actually cutting off service. This is the one quantity this project sets out to prove.
_Avoid_: delay, lag, latency

## Authorization

**Budget**:
The deposit amount a human allocates to the agent when authorizing it. The agent can never spend more than this; it is the entire boundary of the power the human hands over.
_Avoid_: quota, cap, limit

**Delegated key**:
A temporary private key that signs vouchers on the agent's behalf. It is a different key from the human's wallet. The human's wallet signs the deposit once; after that the delegated key signs tick by tick. It cannot touch any money outside the budget.
_Avoid_: session key, burner, private key

## Two layers of identity

**Session**:
The lifetime of one SSE stream. It carries multiple answers and may sit idle in between. Its ID is generated by the buyer and cannot be reused once the session has been cut.
_Avoid_: connection, conversation

**Channel**:
One buyer's deposit account; its lifetime spans many streams. Its ID is derived from the contract configuration. Cutting a session does not affect the channel; once the deposit runs out, no session can be extended.
_Avoid_: account, wallet

**Answer**:
One stretch of inference, from sending the prompt until the last token is emitted. Billing happens only during an answer.
_Avoid_: conversation, turn, request

**Idle**:
The session is alive and the stream is still connected, but no answer is in progress. Idle time is not billed, because no inference is occupying the seller's resources.
_Avoid_: standby, paused

**Cut**:
The seller actively closing the stream because the buyer is overdue. It is the seller exercising enforcement, not the buyer leaving voluntarily. It can only happen while an answer is in progress.
_Avoid_: disconnect, interruption, timeout

**Normal end**:
A session ending without being cut — for example, reclaimed after idling too long. Mutually exclusive with a cut: in a normal end nobody is stopped mid-way; in a cut the seller is exercising enforcement. The two must look completely different on screen.
_Avoid_: end, finish, disconnect

## Roles

**Buyer**:
The party paying for inference; holds the wallet and the deposit.
_Avoid_: user, customer, agent (on its own)

**Seller**:
The party serving inference and charging for it; also responsible for cutting the line and claiming payment.
_Avoid_: server (in prose), provider

**Facilitator**:
A third-party service that verifies payment signatures and submits transactions on-chain on others' behalf; it pays the gas.
_Avoid_: intermediary, settlement layer
